Your home can be threatened by direct flames, radiant heat, and flying embers. Embers can ignite homes up to a mile away.

Roofs: The roof is the most vulnerable part of your home. Homes with wood shingle roofs are at high risk of being destroyed during a wildfire. Opt for composite, asphalt, metal, clay, or tile roofing to resist fires. Close off gaps under roof tiles and shingles to block wind-blown embers. Regularly clear leaves, pine needles, and other debris from the roof to prevent ignition.

Chimneys: Cover your chimney and stove pipe outlets with a non-flammable screen. Use metal screen material with openings no smaller than 3/8-inch and no larger than 1/2-inch. Close the fireplace flue during wildfire season when the chimney is not being used.

Vents: Install 1/16-inch to 1/8-inch noncombustible, corrosion-resistant metal mesh over vents to block embers. Avoid fiberglass or plastic meshes, which can melt. It is recommended to replace vents with California State Fire Marshal-approved flame and ember-resistant vents. Consult your local building official for ventilation requirements before replacing or covering vents and hire a licensed contractor.

Windows: Fit dual-paned windows with at least one tempered glass layer. Limit the size and number of windows facing large vegetation areas. Add screens to all operable windows to catch embers and reduce heat.
